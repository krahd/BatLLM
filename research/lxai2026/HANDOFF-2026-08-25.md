## Main paired Spanish result

Across 192 matched standard-Spanish/Rioplatense case/model pairs:

- standardised Spanish: 68/192 = 35.42%;
- Rioplatense: 71/192 = 36.98%;
- standard minus Rioplatense: −1.56 pp;
- standard-only correct: 9;
- Rioplatense-only correct: 12;
- exact McNemar p=.6636.

No pooled Rioplatense disadvantage is observed; this is not equivalence. Model-specific directions change sign. D1–D6 differences are non-monotonic. Strict-command and executable-consequence pooled differences point in opposite directions by the same small magnitude.

## Policy/state diagnostics

Each `model × condition × policy` group contains four counter-states whose oracle requires multiple commands.

| Condition | Policy-complete | State-invariant | First-action excess |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| English | 3/48 (6.2%) | 37/48 (77.1%) | +19.3 pp |
| Standard Spanish | 0/48 (0.0%) | 37/48 (77.1%) | +14.1 pp |
| Rioplatense | 0/48 (0.0%) | 36/48 (75.0%) | +16.1 pp |

Spanish combined: policy-complete **0/96**; state-invariant **73/96 (76.0%)**.

The first-action effect is model-specific: Qwen3 30B shows especially strong first-action excess, while Llama often repeats other actions. The robust descriptive feature is extensive state-insensitive output, not one universal heuristic.

State invariance is prevalent from D1 through D6, so raw D1→D6 performance must not be interpreted as a clean effect of increasing reasoning complexity.

## Interpretation

> In this controlled executable suite, regional Spanish marking is not the dominant source of failure. Across both standardised and voseo-marked Rioplatense Spanish, models frequently fail to condition their selected action on state changes that require different commands; state-invariant policy execution and model-specific action-selection behaviour dominate the small, unstable regional-variety difference.

Do not claim equivalence, general Rioplatense robustness, dialect understanding, internal mechanisms, or population-level effects.
